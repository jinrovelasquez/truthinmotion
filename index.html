finding my balance: my first time riding a bicycle

Sometimes, learning something new takes more than just watching others do it. It takes patience, courage, and the willingness to try again.

Before the First Ride

Riding a bicycle always seemed easy to me. I had seen many people around me ride without any difficulty, so I assumed that learning how to ride one would be simple. I thought all I needed to do was sit on the bicycle, pedal, and steer in the right direction. I was excited to finally try it myself, especially since I had always wanted to learn how to ride on my own. However, I soon realized that it was not as easy as I had expected. It required more balance, patience, and confidence than I thought.

When I first got on the bicycle and placed my feet on the pedals, I started feeling nervous. I was excited to learn, but at the same time, I was afraid of falling. As soon as I started moving, the bicycle began to wobble, and I struggled to keep my balance. I became tense and found it difficult to control my movements. Watching other people ride made it look effortless, but experiencing it myself was a completely different story.

The Struggle to Find Balance

My first few attempts were not easy. Even with someone helping me, I struggled to gain enough speed to keep the bicycle steady. Whenever I felt like I was losing control, my first reaction was to stop. There were even moments when I became so afraid of falling that I could barely bring myself to pedal. The uneven and bumpy ground made things more difficult, and I almost lost my balance several times.

I also had a hard time coordinating my movements. Whenever I focused on pedaling, I struggled to control the handlebars. But when I concentrated on keeping my balance, I became too afraid to move forward. There were times when I had to stop and start all over again. It was frustrating because I wanted to learn as quickly as possible. However, I slowly realized that riding a bicycle was not just about pedaling and steering. It also required coordination, focus, and confidence.

Learning Through Practice

Even though I was struggling, I did not want to give up. I knew that learning how to ride a bicycle would take more than just one attempt, so I continued practicing and paid attention to the things I needed to improve. I tried to keep my feet in the right position, look straight ahead, control the handlebars, and become more comfortable with the movement of the bicycle.

During the experience, I also received encouragement from the people helping me. They reminded me to stay calm, look forward, and keep trying. There were times when I wanted someone to hold the bicycle because I was not confident enough to ride by myself. Their encouragement helped me feel more comfortable, but I also realized that I needed to trust myself and make an effort.

As I continued practicing, I slowly understood how to control the bicycle better. I learned to notice when it was leaning to either side and make small adjustments to keep my balance. I also realized that being too focused on the possibility of falling only made me more nervous. Little by little, I became more comfortable with pedaling, steering, and moving forward.

Finding My Confidence

After several attempts, I noticed that I was becoming less afraid of the bicycle's movements. I was slowly gaining confidence in keeping my balance and controlling its direction. Although I still had moments when I struggled, I could clearly see that I was improving compared to when I first started.

Eventually, I reached the point where I could ride on my own. It may seem like a small achievement to some people, but it meant a lot to me because I knew how much effort it took to get there. I felt proud of myself for overcoming something that had made me so nervous at first. Being able to ride without depending on someone to hold the bicycle was a great feeling.

What made the experience special was not just learning how to ride, but also knowing that I did not give up even when things became difficult. Looking back, I realized that every attempt helped me get closer to achieving something I had wanted to do for a long time.

More Than Just Riding a Bicycle

My first experience of riding a bicycle taught me more than just a new skill. It made me realize that watching someone do something is very different from actually trying it yourself. Something that looks easy from the outside can require a lot of practice, patience, and effort.

I also learned that making mistakes is a normal part of learning. There were moments when I felt embarrassed because I could not do it right away. However, I eventually understood that nobody becomes good at something without practice. Every attempt, even the unsuccessful ones, helped me understand what I needed to improve. Instead of feeling disappointed, I learned to focus on the progress I was making.

Most importantly, I learned that believing in myself is just as important as receiving encouragement from others. Although the people around me helped me gain confidence, I still had to overcome my own fear and put in the effort. I realized that learning something new takes time, and I should not be too hard on myself when I make mistakes.

Looking Back

Overall, my first time riding a bicycle was a memorable experience because it taught me the value of patience and determination. From being nervous and struggling to keep my balance to eventually riding on my own, I experienced how small improvements can lead to something I once thought was difficult to achieve.

This experience reminded me that I should not expect myself to learn everything immediately, especially when I am trying something new. Instead, I should give myself enough time to practice, make mistakes, and improve along the way.

What started as a simple attempt to learn how to ride a bicycle eventually became a lesson about confidence, patience, and determination. I may have struggled during my first few attempts, but I am proud that I continued trying until I finally learned how to ride on my own. It showed me that sometimes, the best way to overcome a challenge is simply to keep going, even when things do not work out the first time.

I thought all it took was a little pedaling and a steady hand. Turns out, finding my balance required a little patience, a few mistakes, and a reason not to give up.

deadly relief.

You light a cigarette when your head gets loud,
watching the smoke slowly fade into the crowd.
For a moment, your worries seem to slow,
like the smoke takes your problems when you let it go.

It kills your anxiety, it kills the time,
makes everything feel okay for a little while.
The smoke feels good, the silence feels right,
like you finally found peace in the middle of the night.

It clears your head while your body takes the hit,
making you feel better while slowly damaging it.
But isn’t it strange that you feel so light,
while every breath makes your body lose its fight?

So maybe the smoke kills, but not what you thought,
it kills the stress while slowly killing you off.
You breathe in for peace, then breathe out what you lose,
sometimes what feels good, is what you shouldn’t choose.


This was one of my performance tasks for Creative Composition during the first term. I wrote this poem to explore the irony of finding comfort in something that slowly harms you.

The idea behind it is how some people turn to smoking whenever life gets a little too overwhelming. When thoughts get too loud, problems become too much, or anxiety takes over, a cigarette can seem like a temporary escape. For a moment, everything feels lighter, quieter, and easier to deal with. But what happens when the very thing you turn to for relief is slowly taking something away from you?

I wanted to highlight how easily we can become focused on the temporary comfort smoking seems to provide that we forget to acknowledge what it does to our bodies. While we pay attention to the brief sense of calm, we often overlook the physical damage happening with every inhale. The smoke may seem to take away the stress, but it also exposes the body to harmful substances that put our health at risk. In a way, we become so occupied with finding relief from what's happening in our minds that we fail to notice what we're putting our bodies through.

I wanted to portray that idea not only through words but also through actions. That's why I created a video where I played the character in the poem, bringing each line to life through visuals. The cigarette, the smoke, the silence, and the little moments in between were all part of expressing what the poem was trying to say.

For me, Deadly Relief is about the difference between temporary comfort and genuine peace. It's a reminder that not everything that makes us feel better is necessarily good for us. Sometimes, we mistake an escape for a solution, choosing what gives us immediate comfort without considering the consequences that come with it.

After all, what good is finding temporary peace if, along the way, we're slowly putting our own lives at risk?

finding my balance: my first time riding a bicycle

Sometimes, learning something new takes more than just watching others do it. It takes patience, courage, and the willingness to try again.

Before the First Ride

Riding a bicycle always seemed easy to me. I had seen many people around me ride without any difficulty, so I assumed that learning how to ride one would be simple. I thought all I needed to do was sit on the bicycle, pedal, and steer in the right direction. I was excited to finally try it myself, especially since I had always wanted to learn how to ride on my own. However, I soon realized that it was not as easy as I had expected. It required more balance, patience, and confidence than I thought.

When I first got on the bicycle and placed my feet on the pedals, I started feeling nervous. I was excited to learn, but at the same time, I was afraid of falling. As soon as I started moving, the bicycle began to wobble, and I struggled to keep my balance. I became tense and found it difficult to control my movements. Watching other people ride made it look effortless, but experiencing it myself was a completely different story.

The Struggle to Find Balance

My first few attempts were not easy. Even with someone helping me, I struggled to gain enough speed to keep the bicycle steady. Whenever I felt like I was losing control, my first reaction was to stop. There were even moments when I became so afraid of falling that I could barely bring myself to pedal. The uneven and bumpy ground made things more difficult, and I almost lost my balance several times.

I also had a hard time coordinating my movements. Whenever I focused on pedaling, I struggled to control the handlebars. But when I concentrated on keeping my balance, I became too afraid to move forward. There were times when I had to stop and start all over again. It was frustrating because I wanted to learn as quickly as possible. However, I slowly realized that riding a bicycle was not just about pedaling and steering. It also required coordination, focus, and confidence.

Learning Through Practice

Even though I was struggling, I did not want to give up. I knew that learning how to ride a bicycle would take more than just one attempt, so I continued practicing and paid attention to the things I needed to improve. I tried to keep my feet in the right position, look straight ahead, control the handlebars, and become more comfortable with the movement of the bicycle.

During the experience, I also received encouragement from the people helping me. They reminded me to stay calm, look forward, and keep trying. There were times when I wanted someone to hold the bicycle because I was not confident enough to ride by myself. Their encouragement helped me feel more comfortable, but I also realized that I needed to trust myself and make an effort.

As I continued practicing, I slowly understood how to control the bicycle better. I learned to notice when it was leaning to either side and make small adjustments to keep my balance. I also realized that being too focused on the possibility of falling only made me more nervous. Little by little, I became more comfortable with pedaling, steering, and moving forward.

Finding My Confidence

After several attempts, I noticed that I was becoming less afraid of the bicycle's movements. I was slowly gaining confidence in keeping my balance and controlling its direction. Although I still had moments when I struggled, I could clearly see that I was improving compared to when I first started.

Eventually, I reached the point where I could ride on my own. It may seem like a small achievement to some people, but it meant a lot to me because I knew how much effort it took to get there. I felt proud of myself for overcoming something that had made me so nervous at first. Being able to ride without depending on someone to hold the bicycle was a great feeling.

What made the experience special was not just learning how to ride, but also knowing that I did not give up even when things became difficult. Looking back, I realized that every attempt helped me get closer to achieving something I had wanted to do for a long time.

More Than Just Riding a Bicycle

My first experience of riding a bicycle taught me more than just a new skill. It made me realize that watching someone do something is very different from actually trying it yourself. Something that looks easy from the outside can require a lot of practice, patience, and effort.

I also learned that making mistakes is a normal part of learning. There were moments when I felt embarrassed because I could not do it right away. However, I eventually understood that nobody becomes good at something without practice. Every attempt, even the unsuccessful ones, helped me understand what I needed to improve. Instead of feeling disappointed, I learned to focus on the progress I was making.

Most importantly, I learned that believing in myself is just as important as receiving encouragement from others. Although the people around me helped me gain confidence, I still had to overcome my own fear and put in the effort. I realized that learning something new takes time, and I should not be too hard on myself when I make mistakes.

Looking Back

Overall, my first time riding a bicycle was a memorable experience because it taught me the value of patience and determination. From being nervous and struggling to keep my balance to eventually riding on my own, I experienced how small improvements can lead to something I once thought was difficult to achieve.

This experience reminded me that I should not expect myself to learn everything immediately, especially when I am trying something new. Instead, I should give myself enough time to practice, make mistakes, and improve along the way.

What started as a simple attempt to learn how to ride a bicycle eventually became a lesson about confidence, patience, and determination. I may have struggled during my first few attempts, but I am proud that I continued trying until I finally learned how to ride on my own. It showed me that sometimes, the best way to overcome a challenge is simply to keep going, even when things do not work out the first time.

I thought all it took was a little pedaling and a steady hand. Turns out, finding my balance required a little patience, a few mistakes, and a reason not to give up.

deadly relief.

You light a cigarette when your head gets loud,
watching the smoke slowly fade into the crowd.
For a moment, your worries seem to slow,
like the smoke takes your problems when you let it go.

It kills your anxiety, it kills the time,
makes everything feel okay for a little while.
The smoke feels good, the silence feels right,
like you finally found peace in the middle of the night.

It clears your head while your body takes the hit,
making you feel better while slowly damaging it.
But isn’t it strange that you feel so light,
while every breath makes your body lose its fight?

So maybe the smoke kills, but not what you thought,
it kills the stress while slowly killing you off.
You breathe in for peace, then breathe out what you lose,
sometimes what feels good, is what you shouldn’t choose.


This was one of my performance tasks for Creative Composition during the first term. I wrote this poem to explore the irony of finding comfort in something that slowly harms you.

The idea behind it is how some people turn to smoking whenever life gets a little too overwhelming. When thoughts get too loud, problems become too much, or anxiety takes over, a cigarette can seem like a temporary escape. For a moment, everything feels lighter, quieter, and easier to deal with. But what happens when the very thing you turn to for relief is slowly taking something away from you?

I wanted to highlight how easily we can become focused on the temporary comfort smoking seems to provide that we forget to acknowledge what it does to our bodies. While we pay attention to the brief sense of calm, we often overlook the physical damage happening with every inhale. The smoke may seem to take away the stress, but it also exposes the body to harmful substances that put our health at risk. In a way, we become so occupied with finding relief from what's happening in our minds that we fail to notice what we're putting our bodies through.

I wanted to portray that idea not only through words but also through actions. That's why I created a video where I played the character in the poem, bringing each line to life through visuals. The cigarette, the smoke, the silence, and the little moments in between were all part of expressing what the poem was trying to say.

For me, Deadly Relief is about the difference between temporary comfort and genuine peace. It's a reminder that not everything that makes us feel better is necessarily good for us. Sometimes, we mistake an escape for a solution, choosing what gives us immediate comfort without considering the consequences that come with it.

After all, what good is finding temporary peace if, along the way, we're slowly putting our own lives at risk?
